PHẦN I. ĐỌC HIỂU (PASSAGE 1)
Entrepreneurship in multicultural environments offers unique perspectives for business development. These diverse settings bring together people with different backgrounds, languages, and cultural values, creating dynamic marketplaces for innovative ideas. Entrepreneurs operating across cultural boundaries must navigate unfamiliar business practices while leveraging their distinct backgrounds. This cultural exchange often leads to creative solutions that might not emerge in homogeneous environments. Despite the advantages, entrepreneurs face significant challenges when operating in multicultural contexts. Communication barriers frequently arise when team members speak different languages or interpret messages differently. Cultural misunderstandings about business etiquette, negotiation styles, and decision-making processes can hinder progress and create tension. Additionally, regulatory differences between countries require entrepreneurs to adapt their business models and comply with various legal frameworks, which can be costly and time-consuming. On the other hand, multicultural environments present valuable opportunities for growth and innovation. Diverse teams typically demonstrate enhanced problem-solving capabilities by approaching challenges from multiple perspectives. Access to international markets allows entrepreneurs to scale their businesses beyond local limitations. Furthermore, multicultural entrepreneurs often identify untapped market needs that competitors might overlook. These opportunities enable businesses to develop products and services that address specific cultural preferences while maintaining universal appeal. To succeed in multicultural settings, entrepreneurs must develop cultural intelligence and adaptability. This includes learning about different cultural norms, practicing active listening, and remaining flexible when unexpected situations arise. Building diverse networks helps entrepreneurs access local knowledge and establish credibility in new markets. Those who embrace cultural differences rather than merely tolerating them position themselves to thrive in our increasingly interconnected global economy.
